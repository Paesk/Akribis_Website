Research · DLR Agile Justin · 2021

Precision starts with a better model.

Calibration accounts for geometric errors and elastic deformation. In the studied humanoid upper body, researchers reduced the mean absolute end-effector position error from 21 to 3.1 mm.

Read the calibration study ↗

Result for the studied humanoid upper body. This is not a general accuracy specification for humanoids or a measurement of welding-path accuracy.

Further research: calibration with an RGB camera

A follow-up study used the head-mounted camera and visual markers: 3.9 mm mean error and 9.1 mm in the worst case. Different test conditions apply; these figures are not a direct performance comparison with the 3.1 mm result.

Our approach · Unitree G1 · Project start

How much precision can we gain directly at the tool?

We are investigating an additional tool-correction module. The challenge is to detect and compensate deviations quickly within a limited weight, size, and correction range.

Measure the tool position relative to the workpiece and characterise the errors under defined conditions.

02 / Our Approach

Precision sits at the decisive point.

We are exploring a compact add-on actuator at the end-effector to correct small deviations while retaining the humanoid robot's larger range of motion.

01

Detect deviation

Measure the actual tool position relative to the workpiece and compare it with the planned path.

02

Compensate micro-errors

Develop short, fast correction movements to compensate residual errors close to the process.

03

Validate the improvement

Compare tool guidance with and without correction under the same movement and load conditions.
